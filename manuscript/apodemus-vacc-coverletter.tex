\opening{Dear editors,}

We are pleased to submit our final revision of the manuscript entitled \textbf{``Causes of variation in vaccine efficacy in a wild rodent population''} for publication in PLoS Biology.

In this paper we address both the fundamental methodological
issues of transportability, i.e., generalising laboratory-based
results to their intended target population, and we explore the
mechanistic causes for the loss of vaccine efficacy that are
typically overlooked in preclinical phases of vaccinology. To do
this, we used a well-characterised rodent species,
\emph{Apodemus sylvaticus}, which is native to Europe and that
has a characteristically rich parasite community (unlike
\emph{Mus musculus}). The power of this system is that it allows
experimental manipulation as well as longitudinal and
cross-sectional sampling in both the laboratory and natural
populations. In this way, it is unique in its ability to allow
disentangling complex processes that other systems might capture
but be unable to resolve.

The main novelties of this paper include:

\begin{itemize}
	\item Direct comparison of vaccination in laboratory and wild habitats using the same model species, vaccine regimen, and sampling.
	\item Application of structural causal modelling to the identification of the main drivers of variation in vaccine efficacy across laboratory and wild habitats
	\item Elucidation of the relative contribution of habitat, diet, and demographic factors to gains or losses in vaccine efficacy.
 \item Generation of novel insight and hypotheses that might explain some discrepancies between prior reports of the effects of diet and parasite infection on vaccine responsiveness
\end{itemize}

We therefore expect this study to be of broad appeal beyond the
specific biological system in which it was conducted: we bring
further support to the idea that the immune system adopts
alternative strategies depending on diet quality; that parasite
infection interacts with diet in regulating adaptive immunity;
that males consistently generate lower antibody responses to
immunisation.

This manuscript was published on BioRxiv at \url{https://add.url}. In addition to the files submitted for this initial submission, we provide the Overleaf (using the eLife LaTeX template) link to the manuscript: \url{https://www.overleaf.com/read/}

\closing{Sincerely, on behalf of my co-authors,}
